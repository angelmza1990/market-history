Seasonality

Do some months really beat others? The average return of each calendar month, how often it rose, every month on record, and the old saying "sell in May and go away", tested on the data. Seasonal patterns are famous, but most are small next to the month-to-month noise.

Period

Average return by calendar month

Bars: the average return of each month. Whiskers: about two standard errors either side, a rough 95% range for the true average; a whisker that crosses zero means that month's average cannot be told apart from zero. Below each bar, the share of years in which that month rose.

Reading it

Noise is large. A typical stock index moves 4% to 5% in a month, while the gaps between the average months are often below 1%. With a few decades of data, the standard error of each month's average is close to a percentage point, so differences between months are mostly within what chance alone would produce.

Twelve tries. Looking at twelve months and pointing to the best is a multiple comparison: one month in twenty will look significant at the 95% level by luck. A seasonal effect is more believable when it holds in different periods and different markets, which is why the period buttons are there.

Not advice. This page describes the past. Trading on calendar patterns adds costs and taxes, and patterns that become well known tend to fade.
